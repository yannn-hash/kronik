The French Revolution (1789–1799) was a period of radical upheaval that changed the course of French and European history. The absolute monarchy that had ruled France for centuries collapsed in just three years.

## Causes of the Revolution

The crisis in France at the end of the 18th century was triggered by several major factors:

1. **Financial Crisis:** Massive national debt due to France's involvement in the American War of Independence and the lavish spending of the royal family.
2. **Social Inequality:** Society was divided into three Estates. The Third Estate (commoners) bore the heaviest tax burden, while the First (Clergy) and Second (Nobility) Estates were largely exempt from taxes.
3. **Enlightenment Ideas:** Concepts of liberty, equality, and fraternity (*Liberté, Égalité, Fraternité*) from philosophers like Rousseau and Voltaire began to inspire the populace.

## Storming of the Bastille

On July 14, 1789, an angry mob stormed the **Bastille Prison** in Paris. Although it held few prisoners, the Bastille was a symbol of royal tyranny. This event marked the beginning of open popular rebellion and is now celebrated as France's national day (Bastille Day).

> "The Revolution devours its own children." - Jacques Mallet du Pan

## The Reign of Terror

Following the overthrow of the monarchy and the execution of King Louis XVI, the revolution entered a highly radical phase. Led by Maximilien Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety, this period (1793-1794) became known as the *Reign of Terror*. Tens of thousands of people deemed enemies of the revolution were executed by the *guillotine*.

## Legacy

Although the revolution eventually culminated in the rise of Napoleon Bonaparte as an authoritarian ruler, its democratic ideals spread across Europe. The Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen (*Déclaration des droits de l'homme et du citoyen*) remains a fundamental document in the history of human and civil rights.
