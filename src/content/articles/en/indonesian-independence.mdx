On a luminous, reverent Friday morning during the holy fasting month of Ramadan, precisely on August 17, 1945, at 10:00 AM local time, two vanguard leaders of the national liberation movement stepped before an improvised microphone outside a modest residence at Jalan Pegangsaan Timur No. 56, Jakarta. With an unyielding, resonant voice, Ir. Sukarno, flanked by his political partner Dr. Mohammad Hatta, proclaimed a concise two-sentence declaration that permanently altered the geopolitical landscape of the Asia-Pacific: the Proclamation of Indonesian Independence. This historic act inaugurated the sovereign Republic of Indonesia, shattering over three centuries of Dutch colonial subjugation and three and a half years of brutal Japanese fascist military occupation, igniting the Indonesian National Revolution—a heroic armed and diplomatic struggle defending the sovereign republic.

## Historical Background

Indonesian independence was the culmination of an organized modern nationalist consciousness cultivated over decades:
- National Awakening and the Youth Pledge: The founding of modern political organizations including Budi Utomo (1908), Sarekat Islam (1912), Perhimpunan Indonesia, and the Indonesian Nationalist Party (PNI, 1927) culminated in the historic Youth Pledge (*Sumpah Pemuda*) of October 28, 1928: One Motherland, One Nation, and One Language: Indonesia.
- The Japanese Military Occupation (1942–1945): The imperial Japanese invasion expelled Dutch colonial administrators. While marked by forced labor (*romusha*) and resource expropriation, the occupation inadvertently militarized Indonesian youth through the PETA defense militia and elevated the Indonesian language to the sole national administrative tongue.
- BPUPKI, PPKI, and the Formulation of Pancasila: Anticipating military defeat, Japanese authorities sanctioned the Investigating Committee for Preparatory Work for Independence (BPUPKI) in March 1945. On June 1, 1945, Sukarno delivered his historic philosophical address formulating the five foundational pillars of statehood: Pancasila.

Following the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, Emperor Hirohito announced Japan's unconditional surrender to the Allies on August 15, 1945, creating a critical geopolitical power vacuum across the archipelago.

## Chronology of Events

The proclamation unfolded through dramatic tensions between the older and younger revolutionary generations:

1. The Rengasdengklok Incident (August 16, 1945)
Having monitored clandestine foreign radio broadcasts of Japan's surrender, radical youth leaders (Chaerul Saleh, Wikana, Sukarni, Aidit) demanded an immediate declaration of independence decoupled from Japanese-sponsored committees. In the pre-dawn hours of August 16, youth cadres escorted Sukarno, Hatta, Fatmawati, and infant Guntur to a PETA military outpost at Rengasdengklok, Karawang, to isolate them from Japanese administrative influence.

2. Drafting the Text at Admiral Maeda's Residence (August 16–17 Night)
Following senior statesman Achmad Soebardjo's diplomatic intervention guaranteeing their safety with his life, the nationalist leadership assembled at the residence of Rear Admiral Tadashi Maeda at Jalan Imam Bonjol No. 1, Jakarta. In the dining room, Sukarno handwrote the proclamation draft, integrating edits from Hatta and Soebardjo. The text was agreed upon and typed by youth activist Sayuti Melik with minor orthographic adjustments.

3. The Proclamation Ceremony (August 17, 1945)
At 10:00 AM outside Pegangsaan Timur 56, Sukarno read the solemn text of independence alongside Hatta before gathered patriots. The ceremony culminated in the raising of the sacred Red and White flag (*Bendera Pusaka*), hand-sewn by Mrs. Fatmawati, as the crowd spontaneously sang the national anthem *Indonesia Raya*.

4. State Institutionalization and Global Broadcast (August 18, 1945)
On August 18, the Preparatory Committee (PPKI) enacted the 1945 Constitution (*UUD 1945*), formally electing Sukarno as President and Mohammad Hatta as Vice President. Journalists and radio technicians (such as B.M. Diah and Jusuf Ronodipuro) disseminated the declaration globally via Domei radio transmitters and submarine telegraph lines.

5. The Physical War of Independence and International Recognition (1945–1949)
The arrival of British Allied forces and returning Dutch colonial troops (*NICA*) sparked intense armed resistance nationwide (Battle of Surabaya on November 10, 1945, Bandung Sea of Fire, Medan Area). Through a dual strategy of total guerrilla warfare and international diplomatic summits (Linggadjati, Renville, Roem-Van Roijen, Round Table Conference at The Hague), the Kingdom of the Netherlands formally recognized full, unconditional Indonesian sovereignty on December 27, 1949.

## Key Historical Figures

Ir. Sukarno / Bung Karno (1901–1970)
The Proclaimer of Independence, founding President of Indonesia, charismatic orator, and philosopher who articulated Pancasila, uniting thousands of disparate islands into a unified republic.

Dr. Mohammad Hatta / Bung Hatta (1902–1980)
Co-proclaimer, founding Vice President, master diplomat, and the Father of Indonesian Cooperatives whose intellect steered Indonesia's international legal recognition.

Achmad Soebardjo (1896–1978)
Senior diplomat and Indonesia's first Minister of Foreign Affairs whose courageous mediation resolved the Rengasdengklok youth standoff and enabled the midnight drafting session.

Sayuti Melik and Fatmawati
Sayuti Melik typed the definitive proclamation manuscript, while First Lady Fatmawati hand-stitched the original Red and White national flag raised during the August 17 ceremony.

## Impact and Legacy

The Indonesian Proclamation established the sovereign cornerstone of modern Southeast Asia:

- Sovereign Statehood and Anti-Colonial Liberation: Terminated centuries of foreign colonial rule, establishing the legal right of self-determination for over 70 million Indonesian citizens.
- Catalyst for the Asian-African Independence Movement: Indonesia's successful anti-colonial struggle inspired the 1955 Asian-African Conference in Bandung, which accelerated the decolonization of dozens of African and Asian nations and birthed the Non-Aligned Movement (NAM).
- Multi-Ethnic National Cohesion (*Bhinneka Tunggal Ika*): Integrated over 17,000 islands, 1,300 ethnic communities, and diverse religious traditions under the unifying civic identity of Pancasila.
- Independent and Active Foreign Policy: Mohammad Hatta's doctrine of "Rowing between Two Reefs" established Indonesia's non-aligned diplomatic policy of active global engagement for world peace without subordinating sovereignty to imperial superpowers.

## Historical Insights

- The original flagpole used during the August 17 ceremony was improvised from a simple piece of bamboo clothesline pole hurriedly erected in Sukarno's front yard.
- Bung Karno delivered the historic proclamation while suffering from a severe bout of intermittent malaria, having woken up shortly before the ceremony following medical injections by his personal physician, Dr. Soeharto.
- Sukarno's original handwritten draft was discarded into a wastebasket after being typed, but was rescued and preserved in the pocket of journalist B.M. Diah for 47 years before being formally returned to the state archives in 1992.
- The microphone used to broadcast the proclamation was an improvised device assembled from spare parts borrowed from the Japanese Gunseikanbu radio station by technician Gunawan.
