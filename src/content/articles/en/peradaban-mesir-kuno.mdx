The Great Pyramid of Giza is one of the ancient wonders of the world that still survives today. Built during the Fourth Dynasty of the Egyptian Old Kingdom (around 2580 BCE), this monument is a colossal tomb for Pharaoh Khufu.

## Scale and Precision of Construction

This monumental project required extraordinary resources and manpower:

- **Material Volume:** Consists of approximately 2.3 million blocks of limestone and granite.
- **Weight:** Some granite blocks used for the King's Chamber roof weigh up to 80 tons.
- **Labor Force:** Contrary to popular myths about slave labor, archaeologists have discovered that the pyramid was built by tens of thousands of skilled, paid workers.

> "Khufu's pyramid is not merely a marvel of stone engineering; it represents the agricultural and administrative organizational power of Ancient Egypt at its peak."

## Religious and Cosmological Significance

To the Ancient Egyptians, the Pharaoh was a living god (an incarnation of Horus). After death, he would join the sun god, Ra.

1. **Pyramid Shape:** The shape symbolizes the rays of the sun descending from the sky, allowing the Pharaoh's soul to climb to heaven.
2. **Stellar Alignment:** Shafts within the pyramid are precisely aligned with the constellation of Orion and the ancient pole star (Thuban).

## Construction Process

Exactly how the pyramids were built remains one of the great mysteries in archaeology. The leading theory currently involves the use of a ramp system that wrapped around the pyramid as it grew higher. Recent discoveries, such as the papyrus *Diary of Merer*, reveal details about the transportation of fine white limestone from Tura to Giza via the Nile River.
